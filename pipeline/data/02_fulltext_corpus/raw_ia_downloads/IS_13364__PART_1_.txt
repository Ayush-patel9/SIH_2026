FOREWORD 

This Indian Standard was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the draft finalized by 
the Rotating Machinery Sectional Committee had been approved by the Electrotechnical Division 
Council. 

This is a comprehensive standard covering generators coupled to internal combustion engines 
without distinction of output and voltage, with the exception of machines intended for special 
applications, such as machines for traction vehicles, marine service and air transport.

1 SCOPE 

1.1 This standard specifies the requirements for ac 
generators together with their exciters driven by 
reciprocating internal combustion engines and gen- 
erators having outputs up to 20 kVA at specified 
conditions. 

1.2 This standard covers generators with the following 
excitation systems: 

a) Direct excitation adopting static exciters 
(magnetic or electronic); 

b) Brushless AC exciters; 

c) DC exciters; and 

d) Pilot exciters besides exciters in (b) or (c) 
above. 

The excitation power in these cases is derived from the 
prime mover of the alternator. 

The closed loop (involving an automatic voltage 
regulator) or open loop of excitation control may be 
used in the excitation systems. 

1.3 This standard does not cover generators intended 
for use in the following special applications. However, 
machines built to this standard may be adopted with 
suitable modification for these applications when 
agreed between the manufacturer and the purchaser/ 
user: 

a) Exposure to: 

i) abrasive or conducting dust or chemical 
fumes; 

ii) combustible dust; 

iii) dusts/fumes of explosives; 

iv) flammable gases; 

v) lint; 

vi) nuclear radiation; 

vii) oil vapour; and 

viii) salt air. 

b) Operation in mines, entirely enclosed boxes, 
poorly ventilated rooms, damp or very dry 
places. 

c) Operation at speeds other than rated (this 
excludes normal overspeed). 

d) Exposuretoambienttempcraturesabove4(y^C 
or below (PC, 



e) Exposure to cooling media where the tem- 
perature values depart from those listed in this 
standard. 

f) Exposure to abnormal shock or vibration. 

g) Where departure from rated voltage or 
frequency, or both exceeds limits given in this 
standard. 

h) Where the phase voltages, or currents, or both, 
are severely unbalanced. 

j) Where low noise levels are required. 

k) Exposure to external mechanical loads involv- 
ing thrust or overhang. 

m) Subject to operation in an inclined position. 

n) Subject to intermittent, periodic, or varying 
duty. 

1.4 This standard does not apply to the following types 
of machines for which other Indian Standards are 
available: 

a) Turbine type generators (see IS 5422 : 1979), 
and 

b) Electrical generators for use in ships [see 
IS 10242 (Part 3/Scc 1) : 1983].

3 TERMINOLOGY 

3.0 For the purpose of this standard, the following 
definitions shall apply. 

3.1 Machine 

The AC generator to which this standard applies. 

3.2 Rating 

Numerical values of the electrical and mechanical 
quantities with their duration and sequences assigned 
to the machine by the manufacturer and stated on the 
rating plate for specified conditions. 

3.3 Load 

All the numerical values of the electrical and mechani- 
cal quantities that signify the demand to be made on a

mechanism at a given instant. 

3.4 Rated Output 

The apparent electric power available at terminals 
expressed in volt-amperes or kilovolt amperes together 
with the specifies power factor, frequency and voltage. 

3.5 No Load 

The state of a machine rotating at normal speed under 
rated conditions but when no output is taken out of it. 

3.6 Overload 

Any load in excess of rated load expressed numerically 
in per unit or percentage of rated output. 

3.6*1 Sustained Overload 

An overload sustained for a sufficiently long period to 
affect appreciably the temperature of the machine. 

3.6*2 Momentory Overload 

An overload the duration of which is so short as not to 
affect appreciably the temperature of the machine. 

3*6.3 Balanced Load 

A symmetrical 3-phase load having equal numerical 
value of impedance and power factor of load in all the 
3-phases.

3.14 Voltage Terms 

These terms describe a generator under the control of 
its normal excitation and voltage regulation system. 

3.14.1 Rated Voltage (U^) 

The line-to-line voltage at the terminals of the

Range of maximum possible upward and downward

3.14A Inherent Voltage Regulation (RJU) 

The maximum change in voltage expressed as percent- 
age of the rated voltage, when the load changes from 
rated load at rated voltage, speed and power factor to 
any lower load, including no load, without change in 
speed and excitation current, taking into account the 
effect on governor and automatic voltage regulation. 

3.14.5 Transient Voltage Deviation (R^U) 

Maximumdeviationofvoltageafterasuddenspecified

level at rated speed and specified power factor. 

3.14.6 Voltage Recovery Time (Tm) 

The time interval from the point when the voltage 
departs from the specified steady state voltage regula- 
tion band after a load change until the voltage again 
returns to and remains within this band. 

3.14.7 Voltage Regulation Characteristics (— ) 

Curves of terminal voltage as a function of load current 
or KVA at given power factor and rated frequency. 



3jl4*8 Voltage Modulation (Modulation Amplitude) 

Voltage modulation is a quasi-periodic voltage 
variation about a steady state voltage at frequencies 
below the fundamental of service frequency ex- 
pressed as a percentage of the rated voltage at rated 
frequency and uniform drive. 

3,14.9 Angular Displacement of Polyphase System 

Electrical angular displacement of the generator phase 
voltage vector relative to the corresponding phase 
voltage vector of a synunetrical polyphase system 
(with three phase system 120*^ at virtually non-deform- 
ing and virtually t^lanced loads between no load and 
rated load, expressed in electrical degrees). 

3*14.10 Maximum Voltage Unbalance (b^ 

The maximum voltage unbalance is the ratio of the 
negative sequence or the zero sequence voltage com- 
ponents to the positive sequence voltage components at 
no load.

8 IRREGULARITIES OF WAVEFORM 

8.1 Non Linear Loads 

The non linear loads (thyrister drives) introduce har- 
monics in the alternator armature current. This in fact 
disturbs the generator voltage waveform which may 
tead to malfunctioning of the system and also cause 
additional heating of the generator rotor. A standard 
alternator shall supply 20 to 40 percent non linear loads 
along with conventional loads of 80 to 60 percent 
depending upon' the design of thyrister panel and 
alternator parameters. 

It is recommended that users of alternators will inform 
the generator manufacturer whenever the thyrister 
loads or fiimace loads are encountered.

23 OVERSPEED 

23.1 All machines shall be designed to withstand a 
speed of 1.2 times the rated speed unless specified 
otherwise. 

23.2 An overspeed test is not normally considered 
necessary but may be performed when this is specified 
and has been agreed between the manufacturer and 
the purchaser at the time of the order. An overspeed 
test shall be considered as satisfactory if no perma- 
nent abnormal deformation is apparent subsequently 
and no other weakness is detected which may 
prevent the machine from operating normally and 
provided the rotor windings after the test comply with 
the required dielectric tests. The duration of any over- 
speed test shall be two minutes.
