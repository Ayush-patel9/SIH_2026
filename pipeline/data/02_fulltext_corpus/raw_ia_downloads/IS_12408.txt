0.  Foreword 

0.1  In  the  food  industry  the  use  of  gum  karaya  as  an  emulsifier,  stabilizer  and  thickening  agent  has 
been  permitted  under  the  Rules  laid  down  under  the  Prevention  of  Food  Adulteration  Act,  I954. 

0.2  This  standard  covers  gum  karaya,  food  grade.  A  separate  Indian  Standard  has  been  brought  out 
to  cover  gum  karaya  for  use  in  industries  other  than  food  and  pharmaceuticals  [IS  :  5025-1985 
'Specification  for  gum  karaya  ( second  revision  )'  ]. 

0,3  In  the  formulation  of  this  standard,  considerable  assistance  has  been  derived  from  the  FAO  Food 
and  Nutrition  Papers  No.  28.  'Specification  for  identity  and  purity  of  buffering  agents,  salts,  emulsifiers, 
stabilizers,  thickening  agents,  extraction  solvents,  flavouring  agents,  sweetening  agents  and 
miscellaneous  food  additives',  published  by  the  Joint  FAO/WHO  expert  committee  in  Food  Additives, 
Rome  1983- 


1.  Scope  —  This  standard  prescribes  the  requirements  and  methods  of  samplmg  and  test  for  gum 
karaya,  food  grade. 

2.  Requirements 

2.1  General  —  Gum  karaya  shall  be  a  dried  gummy  exudation  obtained  from  the  stems  and  branches 
of  Sterculia  urens  Roxb  and  S.  villosa  Roxb  fam  Sterculiaceae. 

2.2  Description  —  The  material  shall  be  a  white  to  amber  colour,  in  the  form  of  tears  of  variable  size  or 
in  broken  irregular  pieces.  It  shall  possess  a  slightly  acetous  odour  and  a  mucilaginous  and  slightly 
acetous  taste.     The  gum  swells  in  water  and  in  60  percent  rectified  spirit  (  ///  ). 

2.3  Identification 

2.3.1  Swelling  test  with  water  —  Add  2  g  of  the  material  to  50  nnl  of  water  and  allow  to  stand.  The 
material  swells  to  form  a  granular,  stiff,  slightly  opalescent  gel  which  is  acid  to  litmus. 

2.3.2  Swelling  test  with  ethanol  —  Add  2  g  of  the  material  to  50  ml  of  60  percent  ethanol  and  allow 
to  stand.    The  gum  swells  up.    This  test  distinguishes  gum  karaya  from  other  gums. 

2.3.3  Boil  1  g  of  the  sample  with  20  ml  of  water  until  a  mucilage  is  formed.  Add  5  nnl  of  hydrochloric 
acid  and  boil  the  mixture  for  5  minutes.    A  permanent  red  or  pink  colour  develops. 

2.3.4  Shake  1  g  of  the  sample  with  80  ml  of  water  for  24  hours.  Boil  4  ml  of  the  resulting  mucilage 
with  0  5  ml  of  concentrated  hydrochloric  acid,  add  1  ml  or  5  M  sodium  hydroxide  and  filter.  To  the 
filtrate,  add  3  ml  of  potassium  cupritartrate  solution  and  heat.     A  red  precipitate  is  formed. 

2.3-5  Warm  0*5  g  of  the  sample  with  2  ml  of  5  M  sodium  hvdroxide;  a  brown  colour  is  produced, 

2.4  Bark  and  Foreign  Organic  Matter  —  The  material  shall  contain  not  more  than  0*5  percent  by  mass 
( on  dry  basis  )  of  bark  and  foreign  organic  matter  when  tested  by  the  method  of  test  given  in  A-1. 

2.5  Gum  karaya,  food  grade  shall  also  comply  with  the  requirements  laid  down  in  Table  1. 

3.  Packing,  Storage  and  Marking 

3.1  Packing  — The  material  shall  be  securely  packed  in  well-filled  containers  with  minimum  access  to 
light  and  moisture.  The  containers  shall  be  such  as  to  preclude  contamination  of  the  contents  with 
metal  or  other  impurities. 

3.2  Storage  — The  material  shall  be  stored  in  a  cool  and  dry  place  so  as  to  avoid  excessive  exposure 
to  heat.

A-1.  Determination  of  Bark  and  Other  Foreign  Organic  Matter 

A-1.1  Reagent 
A-1, 1.1  Dilute  hydrochloric  acid  —  approximately  4  N. 

A-1 .2  Procedure  —  Weigh  accurately  about  5  g  of  the  material  and  transfer  to  a  250-ml  conical  flask. 
Add  25  ml  of  dilute  hydrochloric  acid  and  25  ml  of  water.  Cover  the  flask  with  a  small  watch-glass 
and  boil  gently  until  the  mixture  loses  its  viscosity.  Shake  the  flask  occasionally.  After  heating  for

chloride  ions.  Remove  the  crucible  to  an  air-oven  maintained  at  105  ±  2°C,  cool  in  a  desiccator  and 
weigh.    Then  dry  to  constant  mass  in  an  air-oven.

A-4.  Determination  of  Total  Ash 

A-4,1  Procedure  —  Weigh  accurately  about  3  g  of  the  material  in  a  tared  crucible,  ignite  at  about

percentage  of  ash  from  the  mass  of  sample  taken.
