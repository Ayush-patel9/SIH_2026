Introduction 

This International Standard shows how information on hand- 
held pneumatic rotary tools, percussive tools and percussive 
tools with rotation should be obtained and presented. 

Such information is valuable for the following purposes : 

a) to enable manufacturers of pneumatic rotary and per- 
cussive tools to offer their products under similar technical 
specifications; 

b) to help users to compare different tools and to select 
the right type and size for a specific task; 

c) to instruct test personnel about how performance tests 
shall be carried out, according to specified conditions 
described in this International Standard. 



1 Scope and field of application 

This International Standard specifies a method of performance 
tests and technical conditions for the supply of pneumatic tools 
and gives detailed instructions on the measurement of power 
output and air consumption and means of adjusting the 
measured values to specified conditions.

pression from the flow condition to the stagnation condition. 

The temperature rise at stagnation of the gas stream can be 
neglected if the gas velocity around the measuring point is 
lower than 30 m/s.

6 Methods for measurement of tool 
performance data 



B.I .3 In general pneumatic tools shall be tested at an effective 
(gauge) air pressure of 6,3 ± 0,15 bar. If the too) has been 
designed for a different pressure (for example 4 bar) this may 
be used and shall be stated in the test report. The working 
pressure shall be maintained under all test conditions. 

6.1.4 The point of pressure measurement (see also 6.2.4) 
depends on the type of tool used. 

6.1.4.1 Rotary tools 

The compressed air working pressure shall be measured im- 
mediately upstream of the tool. 

6.1.4.2 Percussion tools 

With regard to pulsating air flow during performance tests, the 
length of the connecting hose from the point of pressure 
measurement to the tool shall be at least 3 m and preferably 
close to this figure. Hose diameter shall be stated in the test 
report. 

6.1.5 All performance data concerning pressure, number of 
revolutions and blows, power output and blow energy etc., 
shall refer to the same running conditions unless stated other- 



6.1.6 During the test run of the tool, the quality and quantity 
of lubricant recommended by the manufacturer shall be used. 

6.1.7 Due to manufacturing tolerances, even tools of the 
same type give different performance data. To obtain perfor- 
mance data for the type, it is necessary to test a number of 
tools (minimum five) and state the arithmetical average value. 

6.2 Pressure 



6.1 General rules for performance test on 
pneumatic tools 

6.1.1 All measurements carried out in compliance with this In- 
ternational Standard shall be performed by competent persons 
and with accurate instrumentation which is calibrated against 
existing standards or standard methods. 

6.1.2 The performance of pneumatic tools is affected by dif- 
ferent ambient conditions such as atmospheric pressure and 
temperature. Moreover, the temperature of the compressed air 
influences the behaviour of the tool. Test conditions should be 
in the range of values given below : 



— Atmospheric pressure 

— Ambient temperature 

— Compressed air temperature 



960 ± 100 mbar 
20 ± 2 °C 
20 ± 5°C 



During the test run with the tool, the temperature shall be kept 
as close as possible to the test conditions. Any deviation shall 
be stated in the test report. Tests shall be avoided if the at- 
mospheric pressure deviates from the given conditions. 



6.2.1 Accurate measurement of the compressed air pressure 
to the pneumatic tool is of very great importance since the tool 
performance is strongly influenced by this factor. 

6.2.2 For measurement of air pressure, gauges of any suitable 
type could be used. The gauges selected shall be of such size 
and quality that 0,5 % pressure difference of full scale reading 
can be read easily. The pressures to be read shall fall between 
one-fourth and three-fourths of full scale reading. The pressure 
gauge shall be checked and calibrated as often as necessary to 
make certain that sufficient accuracy is attained. For the 
calibration, dead-weight gauges can be used. 

6.2.3 The compressed air pressure to the tool shall be 
measured as total pressure at the inlet of the tool hose as 
specified in the description of the tool, 5.1.7. This means that 
the air shall be at rest without any velocity or have a velocity 
small enough to give a negligible dynamic pressure. This 
means, in turn, that the velocity of the air at the point of the 
supply line where the pressure fs to be measured shall not be 
higher than 15 m/s at 7,3 + 0,15 bar absolute pressure. In 
order to avoid the effect of pressure drop due to supply line 
losses, pressure measurements shall be carried out with the 
tool running.

6.4 Shaft speed 

6.4.1 Shaft speeds shall be measured with good, accurate in- 
struments which shall be checked with sufficient frequency to 
ensure proper calibration. If possible, the total number of 
revolutions during a certain time period should be measured 
using a revolution counter free from slip. 

6.4.2 If the shaft speed is measured according to 6.4. 1 an ac- 
curacy of ± 2 % in shaft speed measurement can be expected. 

6.5 Impact energy 

The measurement of impact energy from percussive tools is a 
difficult problem for which many different methods have been 
proposed. Few of them, however, give a true measure of the 
actual impact energy, (n this International Standard, one 
method has been adopted and is regarded as being suitable If 
properly handled and checked. 

6.5.1 When the hammer piston in the pneumatic tool hits the 
working tool, for example drill steel, chisel, etc., a strain wave 
(strain pulse) is generated in it and travels along it down to the 
bit end. The strain pulse can be recorded with suitable ap- 
paratus. The strain pulse carried a part of the impact energy of 
the piston, and the maximum stress level of the pulse is a func- 
tion of the impact velocity of the piston and hence the energy 
of the piston at impact. By suitable calibration the relation be- 
tween the impact energy of the piston and maximum stress 
level can be determined. For details of this test see annex C. 



6.2.6 It shall also be checked that there are no pressure pulsa- 
tions in the supply line, for example from the compressors. 
Such pulsations might introduce errors in the pressure 
measurement and the measurement of the air consumption. 



6.5.2 For percussive tools with rotation driven by the motion 
of the hammer piston, the rotation mechanism shall be run 
without any torque applied to the working tool during the 
testing of impact energy.

6.6 Blow rate 

6.6.1 The measurement of the blow rate can be made with 
the help of any signal which is clearly related to the number of 
blows of the tool, for example movements of the working tool 
or number of stress pulses in the same, pressure fluctuations in 
the compressed air line in the neighbourhood of the inlet of the 
too! or movements of the working piston or the valve. These 
different pulses can be recorded by means of suitable pick-ups 
and recorders with a time marker. By counting the number of 
signal pulses during a certain time period the blow rate can be 
calculated. 



6.6.2 Since the blow rate of a percussive tool is dependent 
upon the reflections of the impact wave at the bit end, the 
measurement of the blow rate shall be made under normal 
working conditions for the tool. For example, for a rock drill the 
blow rate measurements shall be performed during actual drill- 
ing in rock. 

6.6.3 The accuracy of a properly performed blow rate 
measurement can be expected to be better than ± 2 %.

6.8,1 The air consumption or volume rate of flow when 
measured at the inlet to the tool remains constant when the 
tool Is equally loaded regardless of the inlet temperature or 
small changes in inlet pressure. The air consumption of a tool is 
generally quoted in cubic metres per minute or litres per second 
of free air and is calculated from the measured inlet flow to the 
tool converted to a free air at a reference atmosphere of : 

— pressure : 1 bar; 

— temperature : 20 °C; 

— humidity : 65 %. 

The quoted air consumption can be compared with compressor 
capacity provided the temperature at the tool inlet is the same 
as the compressor inlet. 



6.8.2 The air consumption of the pneumatic tools shall oe 
measured on the inlet side of the tool i.e. in the compressed air 
line to the tool. 



6.8.3 The measurements can be carried out using flow 
nozzles, other pressure differential devices, turbine flow meters 
etc., the accuracy and calibration of which is known. The 
measurements shall be corrected if the conditions in the flow 
meter deviate from those for which it was calibrated. 



6.8.4 With a properly performed measurement of the air con- 
sumption, the result of the measurement can be assumed to 
have maximum deviation ± 5 % from the true value.

Procedure for measurement of impact energy by the strain gauge metliod 



C.I Variant A 

The following method was developed for measuring the blow 
energy of pavement breakers and demolition tools. It can be 
adapted for other percussive tools such as indicated in 6.5.1. 

C.1.1 The essential equipment for the strain gauge method 
is a rod or normal working steel with strain gauge cemented to 
it, a suitable electronic amplifier and recording equipment 
capable of producing the full length of the incident strain wave 
and also of enabling the coordinates of the wave profile to be 
extracted. 

C.1.2 The requirements for the rod are as follows : 

a) the strain-gauged rod shall have a shank conforming to 
ISO 1180 at the struck end and be without area changes. 
The distance from the measuring gauge to the lower end of 
the rod shall be long enough to ensure that the primary inci- 
dent wave is not affected by the reflected wave at the 
measuring gauge station; 

b) the distance from the rod collar to the gauge station 
shall be long enough to ensure that the reflected wave can 
be measured without interference by the re-reflected wave 
from the rod collar. 



0.1.3 There shall be at least two strain gauges diametrically 
opposed on the rod and aligned to measure axial strain. The 
gauges shall be connected in opposite arms of a resistance 
bridge so as to eliminate the effect of strain induced bending. 

C.I .4 The rod shall be well guided relative to the machine so 
that the generation of bending waves is avoided. 

0.1. 5 If a tool is adaptable to two shank sizes, test results 
quoting impact energy should also mention the shank size used 
for the test. 

C-1.6 The absorption device is to be almost refiectionless. 
The reflected energy should not exceed 20 % of the incident 
energy, measured as the ratio between the first reflex and the 
primary incident wave energy to simulate the reflection in an 
actual application, 

0.1.7 The tool operating conditions shall be as follows : 

a) the tool must operate in an upright position on the 
device (see figure 3); 



b) the tool must be firmly held down by a flexible device in 
order to give the same stability that exists under stable 
operating conditions when the tool is embedded in the 
material to be broken up before the latter fractures : the 
flexible device may take the form of calibrated springs or 
pneumatic jacks. (The device used for holding the tool while 
performing the noise level tests to the ECE Directive also 
meets this specification.) The following loading shall be
