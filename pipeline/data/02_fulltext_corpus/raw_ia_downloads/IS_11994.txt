0, FOREWORD 

0.1 This Indian Standard was adopted by the Indian Standards 
Institution on 24 September 1986, after the draft finalized by the Electrical 
Measuring Instruments Sectional Committee had been approved by the 
Electrotechnical Division Council, 

0.2 Mains operated insulation resistance testers are convenient means of 
measuring insulation resistance on production line. They have the 
advantage over hand operated insulation resistance testers covered by 
IS : 2992-1980* or transistorized battery operated insulation resistance 
tester as the hand generator needs table space and both the hands of the 
operator get busy in operating the instrument, and batteries are difficult 
to maintain in daily use. 

0,3 For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this 
standard is complied with, the final value, observed or calculated, express- 
ing the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accordance 
with IS : 2-1960t. The number of significant places retained in the 
rounded off value should be the same as that of the specified value in 
this standard. 



1. SCOPE 

1.1 This standard covers direct reading portable insulation resistance 
testers ( mains operated ) enclosing in the same case a source having a 
rated voltage up to 5 kV dc and moving coil { single coil or cross coil 
type ) instrument for indication of resistance. 

1.1.1 The scope of this standard is confined to mains-operated 
insulation resistance testers.

2. TERMINOLOGY 

2.0 For the purpose of this standard, the following definitions shall 
apply. 

2.1 Rated Output Voltage — The open circuit voltage at the tester 
between the 'line' and 'earth' terminals as assigned by the manufacturer. 

2.2 Rated Resistance — The resistance indicated by the tester 
corresponding to the scale mark, nearest to the scale mark of infinity. 

2.3 Eflfective Range of Measurement — That part of the scale where 
measurement can be made with the stated accuracy. 

2.3.1 The range of resistance value from 1/1 000 up to 1/ 10 of the rated 
resistance value, shall be termed as first effective range and the range 
of resistance from 1/10 of the rated resistance value is termed the second 
effective range. 

2.4 Central Scale Mark — The scale mark corresponding to 1/50 of 
the rated resistance with the scale values 1, 2, 5 or an integral power of 
10 or multiples thereof. 

2.5 Error in Indication — The difference between the indicated 
value and the true value of the quantity being measured. The error is 
positive if the indicated value is greater and negative if it is smaller than 
the true value. 

2.6 Ripple Factor — The ratio of the ripple magnitude to the arith- 
matic mean voltage. 

2.6.1 Ripple — The periodic deviation from the ariihmatic mean value. 
The magnitude of the ripple is defined as half the difference between 
the maximum and minimum values. 

2.7 Indicator — The permanent magnet moving coil indicator or ratio 
type indicator, used to indicate the insulation resistance values. 

2.8 Scale Length — The length of the arc through the tips of the 
shortest scale mark. In case of multiscale instruments, the scale length 
coriesponds to the longest of all scales. 

2.9 Short Circuit Current — The dc current which passes through 
^earth' and 'line' terminals when short circuited if the minimum scale 
marking is zero, and in case minimum scale marking is other than zero 
current which passes through 'earth' and 'line' terminal when a resistance 
corresponding to minimum scale mark is connected. 

2.10 Type Tests — Tests carried out to prove conformity with the 
requirements of this specification. These are intended to prove the 
general qualities and design of a given type of tester.

2.11 Acceptance Tests — Tests carried out on samples taken from a 
lot for the purpose of acceptance of the lot. 

2.12 Routine Tests — Tests carried out on each tester to check 
requirements likely to vary during protection.

11. TESTS 



11.0 General — Tests shall be carried out, unless otherwise specified, at 
the standard temperature of 27 ± 2°G and humidity of 65 ± 5 percent. 

11.1 Type Tests — The following shall constitute the type tests and 
shall be carried out in the sequence given below on the same instrument: 

a) Insulation resistance test ( 11.4 ), 

b) High voltage test ( 11.5 ), 

c) Tests for limits of error ( 11.6 ), 

d) Endurance test ( 11,7 ), 

e) Dust test ( 11.8 ),

a) Insulation resistance test ( 11.4 ), 

b) High voltage test ( 11.5 ), and

11.2*1 Sampling Plan — The recommended sampling plan and criteria

11.3 Routine Tests — The following shall constitute the routine tests: 

a) Insulation resistance test ( 11.4 ), 

b) High voltage test ( 11.5 ), and 

c) Tests for limits of error ( 11.6 ).

tester across all the external terminals connected together and the

Steady reading of the insulation resistance.

11.5 High Voltage Test 

11.5.1 No breakdown, arcing or sparking shall occur when an ac 
vohage of 2 000 volts ( rms ) is applied between all the live terminals 
connected together and the case for a period of one minute. 

11.5.2 The value of 2 000 volts shall be applicable provided: 

1) The maximum steady state output current does not exceed 5 mA 
ac and 10 mA dc or the mixed current 10 mA peak. 

2) The rated output voltage is up to and including 1 000 volts dc. 

In all other cases, the test shall be conducted at voltage equal to 
twice the rated output voltage plus 1 000 volts. 

11.6 Tests for Limits of Error 

11.6.0 Tests shall be done to prove conformity to the provision laid 
down in 9.1 and 9.2 and those given below. 

11.6.1 Error Due to Variation of Level — A change of 5 degrees in the 
level of the tester, in any direction, shall not result in a deviation exceed- 
ing 0'5 mm of the pointer from its initial position. 

11.6.2 Error Due to Variation of Ambient Temperature — The deviation of 
indicator of the tester shall not exceed 5 percent near the central scale 
mark for the ambient temperature change by 4: 20°C from 27°G. 

11.6.3 Error Due to Excessive Humidity — The error of the tester shall not 
exceed the limits specified in IS : 2992-1980* before and after recovery 
after an exposure of 24 hours to a relative humidty of 90 percent and at 
a temperature of 27°C. 

11.6.4 Error Due to Stray Field — The error of the tester at the central 
mark shall not exceed i 5 percent of the indicated value, when the tester 
is exposed to a magnetic field produced by 400 ampere-turns ( dc ) in a 
coil of one metre diameter. The tester shall be so placed as to make the 
influence of the field maximum. 

11.6.5 Ripple at Measuring Terminal — The ripple factor at the 
measuring terminal shall not exceed 10 percent.

of mains operated insulation resistance tester for 10 000 times, there shall 
be no eviiient deterioration and impairment in the woiking of the tester 
and it shall satisfy the requirements given in 9.

11.8 Dust Test 

11.8.1 The chamber of this test shall be capable of maintaining in any 
region inside it, where the tester may be placed, a temperature of 
40 ± 2°G and a relative humidity of not more than 80 percent. 

11.8.2 The atmosphere within the chamber shall be agitated so as to 
keep it loaded with dust particles of such a size as to enable them to pass 
through a wire mesh 150-micron sieve with an aperture of 015 mm. 
The bulk of the material of dust shall consist of free silica. The con- 
centration of dust shall be determined by means of the device described 
in Appendix B where the requirement of dust concentration is also 
specified. 

11.8.3 The tester shall be placed in the test chamber for four hours 
with the atmosphere stirred and the dust shall then be allowed to settle 
for two hours. The tester shall then be removed from the chamber and 
shall satisfy the requirements given in 9.
