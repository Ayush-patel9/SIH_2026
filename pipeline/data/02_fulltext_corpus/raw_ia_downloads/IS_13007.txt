FOREWORD 

This Indian Standard was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the draft finalized by the 
Welding General Sectional Committee had been approved by the Metallurgical Engineering Division 
Council. 

Owing to the development of arc temperatures of the order of 4 000 *^C, a suitable material with high melt- 
ing point is required to be used for the manufacture of electrodes for inert gas shielded arc welding. The 
electrodes should also be a non«consumable. ^ 

Tungsten nieets these requirements. Besides its high melting point, tungsten metal provides an additional 
advantage of high thermo-ionic emission for making such electrodes. This standard covers the requirements 
of such pure tungsten electrodes. 

Addition of substances like thoria ( ThOg ), zirconia ( Zr02^ ), lanthanum oxide ( LaOg ) and cerium oxide 
( GeO^ ) activates the electron emission. The amounts of these oxides may vary from 3 to 4 percent 
depending on the element. 

These dopes are responsible for increasing the usable life of the electrodes, because of their high electron 
emission, belt< r arc starting and arc stability. In addition, the welds have a greater resistance to tungsten 
contamination. Given similar diameters, electrodes containing oxides can take a higher current than pure 
tungsten ones lower diameter electrodes therefore may be used. 

This standard keeps in view the manufacturing and trade practices being followed in the country in this 
field. Assistance has also been derived from the following: 

a) ISO 6848 : 1984 Tungsten electrodes for inert gas shielded arc welding and for plasma cutting and 
welding — Codification. 

b) JIS-Z-3233 Tungsten Electrode for TIG Welding. 

c) AWS 5112 Specification for tungsten arc welding electrodes ( non-consumables ). 

Guidelines for the selection of appropriate grade of tungsten electrodes for a particular application has been 
given in Annex A. 

For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this standard is complied with, the final 
value, observed or calculated, expressing the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accordance 
with IS 2 : 1960 'Rules for rounding off numerical values { revised Y-^ The number of significant places 
retained in the rounded off value should be the same as that of the specified value in this standard.

5.4 Tests 

5.4.1 Chemical composition of the electrodes 
shall conform to the requirements given in 
Table 1. 

5.4.2 Drop Test 

The electrodes shall not break when subjected to 
drop test as described below: 

The electrodes of 3*2 mm or less in diameter 
shall be dropped from the height of one metre 
and that of 4*0 mm and above from the height of 
50 cm. 

The electrode under test, in both the cases shall 
be held 'n' horizontally and shall be dropped on 
to an iron plate of approximately 10 mm 
thickness. 

This test shall be repeated three times for 
identical samples. 

5.5 Arc Test 

The electrodes shall produce performance in 
either polarity in dc and ac applications as the 
case may be. Arc test, if agreed upon by the 
manufacturer and the customer may be carried 
out in accordance with A-5 of Annex A. 

6 INSPECTION AND PACKING 

6.1 Electrodes shall be suitably packed so that 
the surface of the ground finished electrodes shall 
not be damaged during handling and transport. 

6.2 Each bundle or package of electrodes shall be 
clearly marked with the following information: 

a) Indication of the source of manufacturer; 

b) Batch number and manufacturing date; 

c) Electrode grade and colour code; 

d) Size of electrode ( diameter and length ); 

e) Current range; and 

f ) Quantity in numbers. 

6.2.1 The bundle or package of electrodes may 
also be marked with the Standard Mark.
