1.1 Scope 

This Intetnattonal Standard for character shapes and sizes 
is intended to facilitate and foster the use of Optica! 
Character Recognition (OCR) in data processing, by defining 
character shapes suitable for both human and machine 

leading. 

It establishes a common basis for printing equipment and 
optical scanning equipment for OCR interchange appli- 

Cc^itons 

Additional International Standards will cover the print 
Mualitv and the relevant characteristics of the formats 
needed to satisfy mterchange requirements. 

1 2 Field of application 

This International Standard specifies the printed image 
shapes and sizes of alphanumeric characters, graphics and 
symbols designed for use m Optical Character Recognition. 
They are also suitable for general purposes. 

In order to satisfy present requirements and encourage the 
wide extension of OCR applications, two sets of characters 
are specified. These are named OCR A and OCR-B. 

Character set OCR-A includes the numeric sub-set which 
was recommended in diatt ISO Recommendation No 890 
{now part of thjs International Standard) The shapes ot the 
characters have been designed to be suitable for use in 
many applications of OCR Dimensions of OCR-A are given 
in three sizes. 

The shapes of the OCR-B characteis have been designed tot 
use in OCR systems without undue saciifice of their suit 
ability for general purposes in a wide range of applications. 
Dimensions of OCR-B are given m Three sizes. (See part II ) 



1.3 Definitions 

For the purpose of this International Standard the follow- 
ing definitions apply : 

1.3.1 OCR-A : A repertoire of 69 characters of which 56 
ate gtaphics included in the ISO 7-bit coded character set 
(ISO 646-1973). It comprises digits, capital letters, capital 
national letters and other graphics. 



1.3.2 OCR-B : A repertoire of 121 characters comprising 
digits, capital and small letters, all the graphics specified in 
the ISO 7-bit coded character set {150 646-1973), national 
letters, diacritical signs and further graphics. (See part II.) 

1.3.3 printing frame : The smallest rectangle the siaes of 
which are horizontal and vertical and which includes the 
centreline shapes of all characters with the exception of 
long vertical mark. 

1.3.4 si^as of a repertoire : The sizes specified by the 
height H and the width 14^ of the relevant printing frame.

It IS not necessary for OCR purposes that the radii of the corners of 
the nommaJ printed image be less than 0,08 mm (0.003 5 in).
