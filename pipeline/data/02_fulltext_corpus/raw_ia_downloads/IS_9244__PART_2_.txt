PART II PICKING BANDS 
0. FOREWORD 

0.1 This Indian Standard ( Part II ) was adopted by the Indian Standards 
Institution on 20 August 1979, after the draft finalized by the Textile 
Mill Leather Articles Sectional ; Committee had been approved by the 
Textile Division Council. ' ■ 

0.2 The Committee responsible for the formulation of this standard is of 
the view that hairly picking bands have become out of use; therefore, this 
standard does not contain provisions related to such bands. 

0.3 To familiarize the industry with International System (SI) units, 
the basic SI units as well as the recommended SI units for use in the 
textile industry are given in Appendix A. 

0.3.1 The Standards of Weights and Measures Act, 1976 also stipulates 
use of SI units. 



1. SCOPE 

1.1 This standard ( Part II ) covers recommendations on storage and use 
of leather picking bands including those used in jute mills. 

2. RECOMMENDATIONS ON STORAGE 

2*1 The picking bands should be kept inside polyethylene bags and stored 
in a cool and dry place to ensure that they remain pliable. ( A stiff 
picking band springs off the pin on the picking stick resulting in uneven 
picking. ) 

2.2 To avoid fungus problem in the storage of vegetable-tanned leather 
products, use of />-nitrophenol during tanning is recommended; in the 
case of chrome-tanning this problem does not arise. 



IS : 9244 ( Part II)- 1979 

3. RECOMMENDATIONS ON USAGE AND MAINTENANCE TO 
ENSURE OPTIMUM LIFE OF PICKING BANDS 

3.1 The picking band has to be of good quality and proper length, never 
too short. Proper fitting of the pickers to the spindle and proper 
functioning of the picker gear is important for smooth movement of the 
pickers. 

3.2 A rough surface of the picking stick at the delivery end causes 
severe damage to the picking band. Picking sticks should, therefore, be 
checked occasionally for any wear, especially near the delivery end. 

3.3 The picker slot, through which the picking band is fastened to the 
picker, should not have sharp edges* While commissioning a new 
picker, the edges should be rounded. 

3.4 The picking band should be secured to the picker by means of a 
wooden peg. The hole for inserting the peg should be previously 
punched and the slitting of the band to insert the peg avoided. 

3.5 The length of the picking band between the picker and the picking 
stick should be so adjusted that when the loom is at back centre, the 
picker moves freely inward and outward on the picking spindle. 
When the picking stick is at the innermost position, the distance between 
the picker and the buffer should be about 6 cm. 

3.6 A full-length picking band gives longer life than two half-length 
bands. Butt length should be preferred to cut length. 

Note — The thicker butt portion goes on the picker and the other portion goes on 
the picking stick. The butt portion is longer lasting. By cutting the picking band in two 
pieces an advantage lies in its having two longer lasting portions from one picking band 
piece; if one single length is used, this advantage is availed of only once. Further, 
in a longer length picking band the portion on the picking sticks ( which is subject to 
constant frictional contact ) loses its useful properties of elasticity, strength, etc, 
when it gets the chance to perform at the picking band slot on the picker. 

3.7 The picking band should be tied to the picker at its butt end and to 
the picking stick at its shoulder end |n order to ensure longer life. 

3.8 For convenience of recognition, the butt and the shoulder ends should 
be distinctly marked, for example, the butt end may carry two punch 
marks while the shoulder end may carry a cross. 

3.8.1 The butt and the shoulder ends could be marked for full-length 
picking bands. But for bands that have to be cut into two, they should be 
embossed with their brand name on two shoulder ends. Thus, after 
cutting the band into two pieces, the portion with the brand name goes to 
the picking stick and the other portion ( butt ) goes to the picker. 

3.9 The picking band should not strike against the reed cap, buffer edge 
or heald staves.
