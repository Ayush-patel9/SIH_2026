FOREWORD 

This Indian Standard ( First Revision ) was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the 
draft finalized by the Thermal Insulation Materials Sectional Committee had been approved by 
the Chemical Division Council. 

This standard was originally published in 1980. On the basis of the experience gained since its 
publication, the bulk density, and thermal conductivity valves have been modified in this revision 
to achieve a more realistic insulation design. 

Preformed calcium silicate insulation shall be predominantly composed of reacted hydrous 
calcium silicate reinforced with suitable fibres such that the physical requirements, as specified in 
4, are satisfied. 

This standard covers preformed calcium silicate insulation for temperature up to 950°C. However, 
a separate standard, namely, IS 8154 : 1992 'Specification for preformed calcium silicate insulation 
( for temperature up to 650°C ) ( first revision )', has also been published for preformed calcium 
silicate insulation suitable for temperature up to 650°C. 

For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this standard is complied with, 
the final value, observed or calculated, expressing the result of a test or analysis, shall be 
rounded off in accordance with IS 2 : 1960 'Rules for rounding off numerical values ( revised)'. 
The number of significant places retained in the rounded off value should be the same as that of 
the specified value in this standard.

4.8 STANDARD SHAPES, SIZES AND 
DIMENSIONAL TOLERANCES 

4.8.1 Shapes 

Preformed calcium silicate shall be supplied in 
the form of flat slabs, bevelled lags, pipe 
sections or radiused and bevelled lags in comp- 
liance with the requirements of 4.8.1.1 
to 4.8.1.4. 

4.8.1.1 Flat blocks 

These shall be free from warp. All mating 
faces shall be plane and edges shall be square 
to the surfaces and to one another. 

4.8.1.2 Bevelled lags 

These shall correspond with length of standard 
blocks with major width from 75 mm to 166 mm 
in standard thickness of the flat blocks. 



4.8.1.3 Pipe sections 

These shall be in two equal halves, concentric 
and free from warp. The mating face shall be 
plane and ends shall correspond with a plane at 
right angles to the long axis. 

4.8.1.4 Curved segments 

These shall correspond with the diameter of 
the curved surfaces to be insulated.
