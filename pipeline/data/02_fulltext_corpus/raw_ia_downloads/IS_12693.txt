FOREWORD 

This Indian Standard was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards on 19 June 1989, after the draft 
finalized by the Shipbuilding Sectional Committee had been approved by the Transport Engineering 
Division Council. 

The positioning of ships' round windows on ships intended for international voyage assumes greater 
importance for their safe and eflBcient functioning. The safe position of the round windows is depen- 
dent on their strength relative to external forces acting upon the ship. Therefore, there is a need for 
adopting a standard practice for calculating the design pressures to be observed when positioning the 
round windows. 

The formulae for calculation of design pressure is given in Annex A. The graphs for positioning of 
round windows given in Annex B are based on the calculation method for design pressures given in 
Annex A, but are presented in a simplified form for the positioning of round windows. 

In the preparation of this standard, assistance has been derived from ISO 5780 : 1987' Shipbuilding — 
Side scuttles — Positioning', issued by the International Organization for Standardization ( ISO ) which 
takes into account the requirements of International Convention on Load Lines, 1966 ( LL — 1966 ) 
of International Maritime Organization ( IMO ) and Requirement S3 — Strength of End Bulkheads of 
Superstructure and Deckhouses, of International Association of Classification Societies ( lACS ).

1 SCOPE 

1.1 This standard specifies the allowable position- 
ing of ships' round windows applicable for 
passenger and cargo ships intended for interna- 
tional voyages. 

2 CONDITIONS FOR POSITIONING 

2.1 General 

The positioning of round windows shall be deter- 
mined according to the rules and regulations of 
international conventions and codes, national 
authorities and classification societies. 

2.2 Positioning 

The position of a round window on a ship 
depends on: 

a) the position of the round window relative 
to the length L of the ship and to the 
height, V, of its sill* above the summer load 
line, S ( see Graph No. 1 to 4 in Annex 
B ). Where timber load lines are assigned, 
the height shall be measured from the 
summer timber load line to the sill of the 
round window. 

b) The nature and the orientation of the wall 
in which it is fitted, namely, 

1) the ship's side below the freeboard deck, 
and 

2) the fronts, sides and aft ends of super- 
structures and deckhouses. 

2.3 Round Window Position Limitations 

For positions where round windows shall not be 
installed, see 2.3.1 and 2.3.3 and where they shall 
be of non-opening type, see 2.3.2. 

2.3.1 No round window shall be installed in a 
position where its sill would be below a line 
drawn parallel to the freeboard deck on the side 
having its lowest point 2'5 percent of the brea- 
dth, B, of the ship above the summer load line, 
5 ( or summer timber load line, if assigned ), or 
500 mm above the same load line whichever is the 
greater distance. 



*Sill is 
opening. 



defined as the lower end of the glass 



2.3.2 If the vessel is required to satisfy damage 
stability requirements, all round windows whose 
sills are positioned below the final waterline in 
damaged conditions and are fitted outside the 
spaces considered flooded, shall be of the non- 
opening type. 

2.3.3 No round-window shall be fitted between 
the position as defined in 2.3.1 and 2.3.2 and the 
lower part of the curve derived from graph No. 1 
( see Annex B ). 

NOTE — Breadth B in relation to a ship means the 
maximum breadth of the ship measured amid ships 
to the moulded line of the frame in the case of a 
ship having a metal shell, or to the outer surface of 
the hull in the case of a ship having a hull of other 
material. 

2.4 Deadlights 

2.4.1 In ships' sides or in the first tier of super- 
structure or in deckhouses which give access to 
spaces below the freeboard deck, only round 
windows with permanently attached deadlight 
shall be fitted. 

2.4.2 Spaces in the second tier which give direct 
access to closed first-tier superstructures or to 
spaces below the freeboard deck shall be fitted 
with round windows with permanently attached 
deadlights. 

NOTE — For detailed information, see Regulation 
23 ( 7 ) of LL-1966 with relevant interpretations 
adopted by the Maritime Safety Committee of IMO. 

3 STRENGTH LIMITATIONS 

3.1 In addition to the limitations on the use of 
round windows stipulated in 2.3 the lowest per- 
missible position of round windows is dependent 
on their strength relative to the external forces 
acting upon the ship. 

3.2 Calculation of External Forces 

The expected maximum external forces ( design 
pressure ) are found by the calculation method 
given in Annex A. 

3.3 Ultimate Position 

No round window shall be installed in any part 
of the ship where the design pressure exceeds the 
maximum allowable pressure which individual 
types and sizes of round windows can withstand,

B-2 Directions for Using the Graphs 

B-2.1 A scale drawing ( general arrangement plan,

obtained from either Graphs 1, 2 or 3 ). 



B-2,4 A line shall be drawn through the plotted 
ordinates. This line will be the lowest possible 
position of the sill of round windows of the 
particular type when situated in ships* sides or 
in sides of superstructure or deckhouse; however, 
the position is subject to the limitations given 
in 2.3.1 and 2.3.2. 

B-2.5 For round windows in fronts, the chain 
dotted lines in Graphs 1 , 2 and 3 give the addi- 
tional height which should be added to the 
applicable curve referred to in B-2.4. The resul- 
ting height then becomes the lowest possible 
position of the sill of round windows of the 
particular type when situated in the fronts of 
superstructure or deckhouses. 

B-2.6 For round windows of light type, used in 
aft ends of larger ships. Graph 4 gives the actual 
ordinates. The procedure to be followed is the 
same as given in B-2.2 to B-2.4.
