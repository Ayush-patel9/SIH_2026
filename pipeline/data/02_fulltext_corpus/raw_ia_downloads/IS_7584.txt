0. FOREWORD 

0.1 This Indian Standard was adopted by the Indian Standards Institution 
on 25 February 1973, after the draft finalized by the Food Additives 
Sfxtional Committee had been approved by the Agricultural and Food 
Products Division Council. 

0.2 IS: 6120-1971* specifies a method of identification of constituent dyes 
and two methods for quantitative estimation of total dye content. For 
this purpose, ilie methods given in IS : 6120-1971* necessitate the use of 
standard food colours. Therefore, with a view to making available such 
standard food colours to the testing laboratories it was felt necessary to 
have reference food colour boxes. Such a measure, the technical committee 
felt, would ensure obtaining of uniform results of analysis and their proper 
interpretation. It is expected that this standard would facilitate fabrica- 
tion of the reference food colour boxes. 



1. SCOPE 

1.1 This standard prescribes requirements for reference food colour boxes. 

2. REQUIREMENTS 

2.1 General — The reference food colour box shall contain a 20 g sample 
each of the food colours permitted under the Prevention of Food Adultera- 
tion Rules, 1955. The samples of food colours in all the boxes prepared 
at one time shall, as far as possible, be from a single batch of 
manufacture. 

2.2 The box shall be made of plastic or from well seasoned wood. Each 
box shall have provision for keeping not less than twelve samples of 
reference food colour bottles. 

2.3 The bottles shall be made of good quality amber glass or of opaque 
plastic and shall have well fitted cap made of plastic or any other suitable 
material. Capacity of each bottle shall be 40 g. In addition to the food 
colour, the bottle may contain 20 g of coloured silica gel in cheese cloth. 

2.4 Colours — The samples of food colours contained in the bottles shall 
conform to the following Indian Standard specifications:
