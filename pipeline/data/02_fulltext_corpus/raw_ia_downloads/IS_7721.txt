0. FOREWORD 

0.1 This Indian Standard ( First Revision ) was adopted by the Indian 
Standards Institution on 30 January 1986, after the draft finalized by 
the Leather Sectional Committee had been approved by the Chemical 
Division Council. 

0.2 Laces for heavy duty footwear are made from chrome tanned plain 
grain, black leather and are intended to be used in ankle boots. 

0.3 This standard was originally published in 1975 deriving assistance 
from IND/TC 3855 (a) 'Specification for laces, leather, chrome', issued by 
the Ministry of Defence. The present revision has been taken up due 
to recent technological advancements and experience gained during 
implementation oi tne stanaaro. in tnis revision, tue requirement oi 
solvent extractable substance of the lace leather has been included and 
changes have been made in the dimensional requirements and breaking 
strength of the laces. 

0.4 For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of 
this standard in complied with, the final value, observed or calculated, 
expressing the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accor- 
dance with IS : 2-1960*. The number of significant places retained in 
the rounded off value should be the same as that of the specified value 
in this standard. 



1. SCOPE 



1.1 This standard prescribes the requirements and methods of sampling 
and test for leather laces for heavy duty footwear.

2. TERMINOLOGY 

2.1 For the purpose of this standard, the definitions given in IS ; 1640-

3. REQUIREMENTS 

3.1 Laces shall be made from chrome tanned leather obtained from cow 
or buffalo hides/ skins conforming to IS : 578-1985f. 

3.2 The leather shall be given a light coat of dubbin both on the grain 
and flesh sides. The dubbin shall be prepared from mutton tallow, 
castor oil and phenol in suitable proportions. 

3.2.1 Solvent extractable substances of the lace leather when determined 
in accordance with LG : 4 of IS : 582-1 970J and expressed on zero 
percent volatile matter basis shall be 12 percent ( minimum ). 

3.3 The laces shall be cut either by machine or by hand. 

3.4 The laces shall conform to the dimensions and test given in Table 1.

DETERMINATION OF BREAKING STRENGTH 

A-l. The test shall be made on 12 laces, allowing 61 cm gap between 
the grips of the machine in accordance with LP: 6 of IS : 5914-1970'f. 

A-2. After conditioning the samples in an atmosphere of 65 ± 2 percent 
relative humidity and 27 i 2°C for 48 hours, the test shall be carried 
out on a suitable machine in which the test piece shall be stretched at a 
constant rate of 100 ± 20 mm/min. 

A-3. The average result of the tests on 12 pieces shall be considered as 
the breaking strength of the laces.
