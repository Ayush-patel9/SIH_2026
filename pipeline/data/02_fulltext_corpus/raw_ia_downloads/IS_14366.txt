FOREWORD 

This Indian Standard was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the draft finalized by the Food 
Processing Machinery and Equipment Sectional Committee had been approved by the Food and 
Agriculture Division Council. 

It is observed that use of dough mixer has increased in food processing operations. Many times in dough 
mixers problems of shearing and trapping between the tool and the bowl or between the cover and bowl 
are observed. The dough mixer are also subjected to food hygiene requirements. A need was felt to prepare 
this standard to guide the widespread users of the dough mixer in various industries on safety and hygiene. 

In preparation of this standard assistance has been derived from the draft prEN 454 of CEN (European 
Committee for Standardization). 

For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this standard is complied with the final 
value, observed or calculated, expressing the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accordance 
with IS 2 : 1960 *Rules for rounding of numerical values (revised)\ The number of significant places 
retained in the rounded off value should be the same as that of the specified value in this standard.

1 SCOPE 

This standard specifies safety and hygiene require- 
ments for the manufacture of bakery and pastry 
mixers. This standard applies to dough mixers with 
rotating bowls. 

The following machines are excluded: 

— continuously fed machines, 

— mixers with stationary vertical bowls, 

— experimental and testing machines, and 

— domestic appliances. 

These machines are sometimes used in other in- 
dustries (for example pharmaceutical industry, 
chemical industry, printing, ...), but hazards related 
to these uses are not considered in this standard.

3 DEFINITIONS 

For the purposes of this standard the following 
definitions shall apply. 

3.1 The bowl volume is the maximum volume of 
water in litres that the bowl can contain. 



The bowl capacity is the quantity of ingredients 
processed during each operation as indicated in the 
manufacturer's instruction handbook. 

3.2 Description 

A dough mbcer usually consists of: 

— a frame, supporting or containing the driv- 
ing mechanism; and controls (see Fig. 1); 

— a bowl to contain the ingredients to be 
mked. This bowl is rotated either mechani- 
cally or through the action of the kneading 
arm on the dough, may be removable, and 
may tilt; and 

— a device to mix the ingredients which can : 

a rotating kneading arm on a vertical or 

inclined axis, 

two special arms mixing the dough. 

In some cases it can be raised to allow bowl or food 
removal. 

4 LIST OF HAZARDS AND DANGER ZONES 

The standard covers the major mechanical hazards 
which arise during normal operating conditions 
including loading and unloading. It also includes 
hazards due to noise, instability, dust, as well as 
hygiene requirements. 

4.1 Normal Operating Conditions 

Risk analysis under 5.3 to 5,8 are based on the 
normal operating conditions of dough mixers 
which is the operation of processing various 
ingredients by means of kneading arm. 

The range of processed ingredients is extremely 
wide. Operation is usually carried out in cycles of 
variable duration. 

Manual operations are sometimes necessary to 
take samples, to scrape or add ingredients. 

4.2 Other Operating Conditions 

If the machine is not used as above, the 
manufacturer should, when he is informed of such 
situation, check on the basis of a new risk analysis 
that preventive measures remain valid.

5 SAFETY REQUIREMENTS AND SAFETY 
MEASURES 

5*1 Mechanical Hazards 

5.1.1 Zone 1 — Volume Covered by the Tool 

5.1.1.1 Direct access to the volume covered by the 
kneading arm shall be prevented by an interlocking 
guard. 

An opening to see and take dough samples during 
the kneading process is allowed and it shall be 
entirely within the hatched area shown in Fig. 3. 

This hatched area shall be located on the bowl side 
opposite the kneading arm zone, and it shall have 
the following dimensional characteristics; 

— if dimension V is 120 mm or less no opening 
'a' shall be allowed. 

— if dimension *Z>' is over 120 mm and equal to 
230 mm or less, dimension 'a' shall be 100 
mm or less, 

— if dimension 'b' is over 230 mm, dimension 
'a' shall be 140 mm or less, 

— clearance 'o* between the bowl protective 
cover and the bowl edge or between the bowl 
guard and the rotating bowl edge shall 
always be less than 25 mm, 

— distance y between the bowl and the guard, 
before the mechanism is stopped shall not 
exceed: 



25 mm if the dimension b is 120 mm or 
less, 

50jnm if the dimension7> is over 120 
mm and up to 230 mm 

75 mm if the dimension b is over 230 
mm. 

5.1.1.2 In order to facilitate dough discharge from 
dough mixers with non removable bowls, the 
kneading arm and bowl motions can continue when 
the guard is open, by operation of a hold-to-run 
control. 

5.1.1.3 Stopping time 

When operated the stopping devices shall stop the 
dough mbcer within a maximum of 4 second. If this 
is not possible opening of the guard should be 
prevented until movement has ceased by a time 
delay device. 

5.1.2 Zone 2 — Space Between Bowl and Frame 

The clearance between the rotating bowl and the 
machine stationary parts shall be at least 30 mm, 
unless design prevents access (for example 
clearance less than 4 mm). 

5.1.3 Zone 3 — Bowl Driving Mechanisms 

5.1.3.1 An interlocking device shall prevent the 
bowl driving mechanism and the kneading arm 
from moving, when the bowl is not in operating 
position.

5.1.3.2 Tilting bowls 

If tilting mechanical, it shall be operated by a hold- 
to-run control. If tilting is manual, it shall require 
a force not over 350 N. 

5.1.4 Zone 4 — Kneading Arm Driving Mechanism 

5.1.4.1 The kneading arm shall not rotate, unless it 
is in operating position within the bowl. 

5.1.4.2 Kneading arm lowering when mechanical, 
shall be controlled by a hold-to-run control. 

When manual, kneading arm lowering or lifting 
with the bowl empty shall require a force not over 
350 N in normal operating conditions. 

5.2 Electrical Hazards 

The electrical equipment shall be in accordance 
with IS 1356 (Part 1) : 1972. 

Control devices and indicators shall have a degree 
of protection IP 54. 

The whole machine shall have a degree of pro- 
tection IP 24. 

5.3 Noise Measurement 

Measurements shall be carried out in compliance 
with the standard IS 12065 : 1987 in conditions of 
free field over a reflecting plane. 

The test shall be carried out with an empty bowl at 
different speeds in each gear. If a continuous varia- 
tion of speed is used, at least three speeds, covering 
the complete range, shall be selected including the 
fast one. 

The A-weighted equivalent continuous sound 
pressure level shall be measured at the operator's 
station. 

5.4 Protection Against Flour Dust Emission 

For a bowl with a diameter more than 500 mm, 
protection against flour dust emission shall be 
ensured by either of the following means. 

5.4.1 Use of a solid cover (apart from the opening 
allowed under 5.1.1.1). 

5.4.2 It is recommended that by means of a time- 
delay device which, when starting the machine, 
makes the tool rotate in lowest speed during at least 
120 seconds before the operator can engage a 
higher speed. 

5.5 Loss of Stability 

Machines shall be as far as possible designed to be 
stable and shall comply with the requirements set 
out in 5.5.1 and 5,5.2. 



5.5.1 Unfixed standing machines without castors 
shall pass the following test. The machine shall be 
titled 10^ from the horizontal plane in the most 
unfavourable direction with the bowl filled with 
water, up to the nominal capacity. It shall return to 
the horizontal plane. 

5.5.2 Unfixed standing machines with castors shall 
have at least two castors (or sets of castors) fitted 
with a locking device, if necessary, and shall comply 
with the provisions of 5.5.1. 

5.5.3 If the machine cannot comply with the above 
requirements, it shall be fixed and the instruction 
handbook shall indicate the value of forces at fking 
points. 

5.6 Hygiene Requirements 

The dough mixers shall be designed and manufac- 
tured in accordance with the definition given in 
Annex A and Annex B. 

6 INFORMATION FOR USE 

6.1 Marking 

The following shall be marked on the machine: 

— name of the manufacturer and/or seller or 
hirer, 

— type of machine, 

— serial number, 

— year of construction, 

— data on power supply : voltage in volts, 
frequency in hertz and power consumption 
expressed in kilowatts, and 

— sound power level if necessary 

6.2 Instruction Handbook 

This handbook shall be written in the official 
language(s) of the country where the machine is 
intended to be used. 

Units shall be SI units. 

The instruction handbook shall include the 
following. 

6.2.1 Technical Data 

— general description of the machine with 
dimensions sketches, etc, 

— normal operating conditions, 

— machine weight and component weights if 
the machine is supplied in parts, 

— slinging and handling precautions, 

— loads on fixing points, if the machine is to be 
fbced, and 

— wiring diagram. 

6.2.2 Operation Manual 

6.2.2. 1 Normal operating conditions

The operation manual shall be adapted according

6.2.3 Maintenance Manual 



This manual shall include:

ANNEX A 

(Clause 5,6) 

DEFINITION OF THE VARIOUS MACHINE ZONES IN TERMS 
OF 'HYGIENE REQUIREMENTS' 



A-1 FOOD CONTACT ARFA 

This area is composed of any surface usually in 
contact with food products; the food area also 
includes the surface with which the product may 
incidentally come into contact, Bfter which it 
returns into the main product under normal condi- 
tions of use. 



A-2 SPLASH AREA 

Any surface of the equipment on which part of the 
food product may incidentally splash or flow along 
under normal condition of use and does not return 
into the main product flow. 

A-3 NON-FOOD CONTACT AREA 

Any exposed surface other than those specified 
above.
