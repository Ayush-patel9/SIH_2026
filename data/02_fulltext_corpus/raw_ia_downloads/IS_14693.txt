FOREWORD 

This Indian Standard was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the draft finalized by the Machine 
Tool Elements and Holding Devices Sectional Committee had been approved by the Production Engineering 
Division Council. 

The continuous research in the area of enhancing productivity has given rise to better machine tools and the 
efforts to reduce fatigue of workmen has led the development of workholding devices operated by power. 

The power operated chuck reduces the loading and unloading time and leads to higher productivity, than achieved 
by manually operated chucks.

3 TERMINOLOGY 

For the purpose of this standard, the terminology as 
given below shall apply (see also Fig. 1). 

3.1 Chuck 

Device to be mounted on the spindle nose of a 
machine tool for holding a work piece or a tool 
(directly or with an adapter). 

3.2 Power Operated Self Centering Chucks 

A chuck in which all the jaws move simultaneously 
towards or away from the axis of rotation. 

This chuck is operated by hydraulic or pneumatic or 
electrical power actuator linked to chuck operating 
mechanism to move the jaws simultaneously inward 
or outward, radially to grip the work piece, concentric 
to the axis of rotation.

3.3 Chuck Body 

The part of the chuck which encloses operating mecha- 
nisms, and jaws, and also provides for mounting. 

3.3.1 Chuck Face 

The face from which the master jaws protrude. 

3.3.2 Back Face 

The face of chuck that contacts the face of the 
spindle or adaptor when chuck is mounted on the 
machine. 

3.4 Master Jaw (Base Jaw) 

The part formed to fit in the jaw slot of the chuck body 
and provides for mounting top jaw either hard or 
soft. Master jaws once fitted in a body shall not be 
interchanged. 

3.5 Top Jaw 

3.5.1 The work holding member, intended to be 
mounted and clamped on the master jaw. 

3.5.2 The top jaw may be either hard or soft. 

3.5.2.1 Hard jaws have to be ground in position. Once 
so ground, the jaws shall not be interchanged. These 
jaws are reversible. 

3.5.2.2 Soft jaws have to be machined in position. 
Once removed, these jaws have to be remachined 
after refitment. 

3.6 Jaw Actuator 

This member of the chuck is connected to power 
source and moves the base jaw through chuck operat- 
ing mechanism. 

4 MATERIAL 

4.1 Body 

Medium carbon steel having minimum hardness of 1 90 
HB to 220 HB Jaw guide ways shall be hardened to 
46 HRC to 52 HRC. 

4.2 Master Jaw (Base Jaw) 

Suitable steel case hardened to a hardness of 54 HRC 
to 60 HRC. 

4.3 Jaw Actuator (Wedge) 

Suitable steel case hardened to a hardness of 54 HRC 
to 60 HRC.

8 TESTING 

8.1 Geometrical Test 

8.1.1 Preliminary Remarks 

Since all the geometrical tests to be carried out involve 
the chuck rotation, the chuck should be mounted on 
the suitable test spindle. The mounting of the chuck 
on the spindle nose shall be made either directly on 
the spindle nose or with an adapter placed between 
the spindle nose and the chuck. The flange or the adap- 
tor shall have been already tested for the accuracy of 
size, concentricity with the spindle axis and absence 
of face out of the flange or adaptor. 

8.1.2 Spindle A ccuracy 

The spindle accuracy shall be tested according to 
IS 2063 and 8.2.11 of IS 2876. 

8.2 Concentricity of Outside Diameter 

When a chuck is mounted on spindle as shown in Fig. 2, 
the concentricity of the outside diameter shown by a 
dial indicator reading shall be within the limits as given 
in Table 3. 

8.3 Accuracy of Grip on Test Bar 

The accuracy required shall be as given in Table 4.

on the mandrel at a distance ' /' from the jaw. The dial 
gauge reading shall not exceed the maximum values 
given in Table 4. 

8.3.2 The accuracy of test bar to be used shall 
conform to the requirements given in A-l of IS 2063.

The static balance of the chuck shall be tested in the

shall then be mounted on the adaptor and the mandrel 
placed on knife-edges as shown in Fig. 3. The weight 
required to effect the static balance for the self 
centering chuck shall conform to the limits specified 
in Table 7 and the arrangement is given in Fig. 3. The 
test shall be carried out only when the chuck is 
completely dry inside and without top jaws, T-nuts/ 
Hexagon socket cap screws.
