FOREWORD 

This  Indian  Standard  (First  Revision  )  was  adopted  by  the  Bureau  of  Indian  Standards,  after  the 
draft  finalized  by  the  Surgical  Dressings  Sectional  Committee  had  been  approved  by  the  Medical 
Equipment  and  Hospital  Planning  Division  Council. 

This  standard  was  first  published  in  1984.  The  first  revision  of  this  standard  has  been  taken  up 
to  align  it  with  British  Pharmacopoeia  in  respect  of  the  requirement  of  X-ray  opacity  which  was 
not  specified  in  the  earlier  version. 

X-ray  detectable  gauze  swabs  and  laparotomy  sponges  are  widely  used  items  in  operation  theatres 
of  hospitals.  The  presence  of  X-ray  detectable  member  enables  them  to  be  located  in  case  of 
their  being  left  inside  the  body  accidentally. 

For  the  purpose  of  deciding  whether  a  particular  requirement  of  this  standard  is  complied  with, 
the  final  value,  observed  or  calculated,  expressing  the  result  of  a  test  or  analysis,  shall  be 
rounded  off  in  accordance  with  IS  2  :  1960  'Rules  for  rounding  off  numerical  values  (  revised )'. 
The  number  of  significant  places  retained  in  the  rounded  off  value  should  be  the  same  as  that  of 
the  specified  value  in  this  standard.

3.1  X-ray  detectable  gauze  swabs  and  laparo- 
tomy sponges  shall  consist  -of  folded  absorbent 
gauze,  to  which  is  attached  a  non-toxic  and 
distinctively  coloured  monofilament  or  "multi- 
filament X-ray  detectable  ^member.  The 
absorbent  gauze  shall  consist  of  cotton  -cloth 
of  plain  or  leno  weaves,  bleached  to  white, 
clean  and  reasonably  free  from  weaving  defects, 
cotton  leaf  and  shell.  The  gauze  shall  be 
almost  odourless.  The  X-ray  detectable  mem- 
ber shall  be  distinctively  coloured  and  reasona- 
bly free  from  loose  fibres  and  particles;  when 
attached,  it  shall  not  impair  the  softness  or 
flexibility  of  the  gauze. 

NOTE  —  The  X-ray  detectable  member  consists  of 
not  less  than  55  percent  of  barium  sulphate  or  a 
quantity  of  any  other  suitable  material  giving  a  com- 
parable X-ray  opacity.

5  MANUFACTURE,  WORKMANSHIP  AND 
FINISH 

5.1  The  gauze  swabs  shall  be  made  by  folding 
the  absorbent  gauze  to  the  required  size  in  such 
a  way  that  the  free  edges  do  not  iprotrude  from 
the  sides.  The  X-ray  detectable  member  shall 
be  suitably  fixed  to  the  internal  folds  of  the 
gauze  in  a  longitudinal  direction.  The  maximum 
off-fold  or  the  misalignment  for  an  individual 
swab  shall  not  be  more  than  0*5  cm.  It  shall 
be  clean  and  free  from  physical  damage. 

5.2  In  case  of  laparotomy  sponges,  the  swabs 
shall  be  made  by  folding  the  absorbent  gauze 
to  the  required  size  and  then  stitching  on  all 
four  sides  with  loose  ends  folded  inwards.  The 
X-ray  detectable  member  shall  be  suitably 
placed  at rthe  centre  of  the  fold  at  one  corner 
and  stitched  from  all  the  sides.  A  suitable 
braided  cotton  tape  may  be  attached  at  one 
end  of  the  laparotomy  sponge  (  the  recommen- 
ded width  and  length  of  braided  cotton  tape  is 
10  mm  and  300  mm,  respectively  )..  The  maxi- 
mum off- fold  or  misalignment  of  the  fold  shall 
not  be  more  than  10  mm.  The  swab  which  shall 
be  clean  and  free  from  physical  damage,  shall 
also  be  stitched  with  two  cross  stitches  at  the 
centre  of  the  swab,  parallel  to  the  sides. 

5.3  The  X-ray  detectable  member  shall  be 
properly  anchored  to  the  gauze.  It  shall  show 
no  sign  of  damage  or  brittleness. 

5.4  The  length  of  the  X-ray  detectable  member 
shall  be  according  to  the  size  of  the  swab.  It 
shall  be  along  the  entire  length  of  the  swab. 
The  thickness  of  the  X-ray  detectable  member 
shall  not  be  less  than  0-5  ram.

and  laparotomy  sponges  shall  conform  to  the

6  OPACITY 

6.1  The  gauge  swabs  and  laparotomy  sponges 
shall  satisfy  the  test  for  X-ray  opacity  in 
accordance  with  the  method  specified  in  British 
Pharmacopoeia,  Ed.  1988,  Vol  II,  Appendix 
XXR,  (M228  ). 

7  STERILIZATION 

7.1  If  the  product  is  to  be  supplied  in  sterilized 
condition,  it  may  be  sterilized  by  any 
suitable  method  of  sterilization  in  accordance 
with  IS  10150  :  1981. 

7.1.1  The  sterilized  X-ray  detectable  gauze 
swabs  and  laparotomy  sponges  shall  comply 
with  the  test  for  sterility  described  in  Apendix  2$ 
(p9\7)  in  of  British   Pharmaceutical  Codex, 

1973.

of  the  product.  The  generic  name  shall 
be  marked  more  conspicuously  than  the 
brand  name; 


b).  Name  of  the  dye  used  for  colouring  of 
X-ray  detectable  member; 

c)  Overall  dimensions  and  number  of  plies

quantity; 

d)  Batch/lot  number  and  date  of 
manufacture; 

e)  Indication  of  the  source  of  manufacture; 

f)  The  country  of  manufacture;  and 

g)  If  sterilized,  it  shall  be  described  as 
'STERILIZED  —  Sterility  guaranteed 
unless  the  pack  is  opened  or  damaged'. 

9  PACKING 

9.1  The  X-ray  detectable  gauze  swabs  and 
laparotomy  sponges  shall  be  packed  in  either 
well-closed  packages  or  sealed  packages 
according  to  the  requirements  of  the  user.  The 
wax  paper  shall  not  be  used  for  any  wrapping 
as  it  affects  the  absorbency  of  the  gauze. 

9.1.1  If  the  material  is  sterilized,  it  shall  be 
enclosed  in  a  sealed  package  which  is  adequate 
to  maintain  the  sterility  of  the  material   up   to 

the  time  of  opening  the  package. 


Standard  Mark 

The  use  of  the  Standard  Mark  is  governed  by  the  provisions  of  the  Bureau  of  Indian 
Standards  Act,  198b  and  the  Rules  and  Regulations  made  thereunder.  The  Standard  Mark 
on  products  covered  by  an  Indian  Standard  conveys  the  assurance  that  they  have  been 
produced  to  comply  with  the  requirements  of  that  standard  under  a  well  defined  system 
of  inspection,  testing  and  quality  control  which  is  devised  and  supervised  by  BIS  and 
operated  by  the  producer.  Standard  marked  products  are  also  continuously  checked  by

which  a  licence  for  the  use  of  the  Standard  Mark  may  be  granted  to  manufacturers  or
